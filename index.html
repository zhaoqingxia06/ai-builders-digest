Training Data：Box's Aaron Levie: On Reinventing Yourself in the AI Age and Enterprise Diffusion

一句话要点：应用公司正在成为最热的 AI 实验室，而企业的胜负手在于「模型能力」与「真实工作流」之间的那座桥。

Box CEO Aaron Levie 在 Sequoia 的 Training Data 播客里给出的核心判断是——过去被嘲笑的「模型 wrapper」恰恰是当下最成立的生意，因为企业真正购买的不是模型能力本身，而是从模型能力到既有工作流之间的那座桥。Levie 的证据来自 Box 内部：一个接入搜索、文件系统、嵌入检索的 agent harness，能对二十年积累的数千万份文件随问随答——多项搜索、重排序、再通读全文，在准确率和延迟上都显著优于直接调用通用模型 API。「我们确切知道该怎么做，」他反复强调，这种领域理解就是护城河。节目开篇他还给在校学生提了一个朴素建议：关注对的那 20 个账号、真正用起来——「你的信息流决定你领先还是落后一年。」

When Humans Adopt Rational Agents

Box's Aaron Levie posts twice in one day — predicting agents will make the optimal choice for users and talking enterprise reinvention on Sequoia's podcast; meanwhile "code freeze" gets declared dead and Vercel moves a whole browser into Rust.
A 7-min read · Follow Builders

Daily Insights

Core insight: both of today's main threads are about what happens when AI actually gets used — Every CEO Dan Shipper notes the irony of an industry built on modeling humans as rational agents panicking as humans adopt rational agents, while Box CEO Aaron Levie gives the enterprise answer in a tweet and a podcast: agents should make the optimal choice for users, and companies must actively reinvent themselves for the AI age. The engineering signals were just as dense — "code freeze" was declared dead by OpenAI's Codex team, Vercel moved a whole browser into Rust, and Steinberger started rebuilding CI.

TWITTER

Box CEO Aaron Levie raises a question every industry should think about: what happens when agents begin to make the best or most efficient choice for their users — the part of the economy that benefits from the friction customers traditionally have when changing habits will be directly exposed to the agent's optimal answer. The same week, he joined Sequoia's Training Data podcast to talk about Box's reinvention with AI.

Training Data: Box's Aaron Levie: On Reinventing Yourself in the AI Age and Enterprise Diffusion

The takeaway: app companies are becoming the hottest AI labs, and the real business is the bridge between model capability and the messy workflows enterprises already have.

Box CEO Aaron Levie's core judgment on Sequoia's Training Data podcast — the once-mocked "model wrapper" is now the most defensible business, because what enterprises buy is not raw model capability but the bridge from that capability to their existing workflows. His evidence comes from inside Box: an agent harness with access to search, file systems and on-the-fly embeddings answers questions over tens of millions of files accumulated across twenty years — running multiple searches, reranking, then reading the full document — with meaningfully better accuracy and latency than calling a general-purpose model API directly. "We know exactly how to do that," he stresses; that domain understanding is the moat. He also offered students a plain early-career tip: follow the right twenty accounts and actually use the platform — your feed decides whether you're a year ahead or a year behind.

With 76% of agentic e-commerce under 30 cents, card rails are out and Coinbase goes all in on stablecoins; Muse sprints ahead of Siri in the entry race, while agents start QA-ing their own software.

Daily Insights

Core insight: nearly everything today points at the same shift — agents graduating from demo to actual economic actors. The entry layer is fighting over who becomes the default personal agent (Muse, ChatGPT, Siri), the finance layer is giving agents accounts and settlement rails (stablecoins, X402), and the engineering layer is letting agents build the software themselves (recursive self improvement, agent-driven QA). All three moving at once is what an "agent infrastructure year" looks like.

PODCASTS

No Priors: Coinbase's Everything Exchange: Agentic Finance, Stablecoins, and Tokenization with CEO Brian Armstrong (2026-09-10)

The takeaway: once AI agents start spending their own money, card rails (a 30¢ flat fee per transaction) become the ceiling — stablecoins plus self-custodial wallets are the ready-made low-cost global settlement layer, and Coinbase is going all in on it.

Brian Armstrong, Coinbase's cofounder and CEO (and cofounder of the longevity company New Limit), grounds the argument in a striking stat: about 76% of agentic e-commerce transactions are under 30¢, while the typical debit or credit minimum fee is 30¢ flat plus a percentage — traditional rails simply don't work at that size, and stablecoins move money anywhere in the world in under a second for under 1¢. Coinbase now lets any agent get its own financial account from a single pasted prompt (a self-custodial wallet, no KYC — agents have no government ID), and the agent payments protocol it incubated, X402, has been donated to the Linux Foundation with Google, Cloudflare, and AWS working on it. "We don't want the AIs to be unbanked," he says, predicting there will be more agents than humans in the not too distant future, and that the agentic economy will eventually be bigger than the human one.

Inside Coinbase engineering, every team, repository, and even individual gets a "brain" — the full history of incidents, financial controls, A/B tests, and accepted or rejected PRs. When a human corrects an agent's change, that correction has to flow back into the brain, so the one-shot PR acceptance rate keeps ticking up: what he calls recursive self improvement. Using the internal harness Toshi, he now breaks a feature plan into phases, spins up 10 parallel agents (mostly cheaper models) to execute, and gets a completion ping minutes later — the CEO is shipping features himself again.

By the numbers: 88% of Coinbase revenue already comes from non-Bitcoin trading; prediction markets hit a $100M revenue run rate within months of launching; tokenized stocks (real securities held 1:1 in custody, available outside the US first) target the roughly 4 billion people without brokerage access. At New Limit, epigenetic reprogramming has successfully reprogrammed at least one human cell type in humanized mouse models, with the first phase-one clinical trial launching next year in alcoholic liver disease.
