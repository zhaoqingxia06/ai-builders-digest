No Priors: Coinbase's Everything Exchange: Agentic Finance, Stablecoins, and Tokenization with CEO Brian Armstrong (2026-09-10)

The takeaway: once AI agents start spending their own money, card rails (a 30¢ flat fee per transaction) become the ceiling — stablecoins plus self-custodial wallets are the ready-made low-cost global settlement layer, and Coinbase is going all in on it.

Brian Armstrong, Coinbase's cofounder and CEO (and cofounder of the longevity company New Limit), grounds the argument in a striking stat: about 76% of agentic e-commerce transactions are under 30¢, while the typical debit or credit minimum fee is 30¢ flat plus a percentage — traditional rails simply don't work at that size, and stablecoins move money anywhere in the world in under a second for under 1¢. Coinbase now lets any agent get its own financial account from a single pasted prompt (a self-custodial wallet, no KYC — agents have no government ID), and the agent payments protocol it incubated, X402, has been donated to the Linux Foundation with Google, Cloudflare, and AWS working on it. "We don't want the AIs to be unbanked," he says, predicting there will be more agents than humans in the not too distant future, and that the agentic economy will eventually be bigger than the human one.

Inside Coinbase engineering, every team, repository, and even individual gets a "brain" — the full history of incidents, financial controls, A/B tests, and accepted or rejected PRs. When a human corrects an agent's change, that correction has to flow back into the brain, so the one-shot PR acceptance rate keeps ticking up: what he calls recursive self improvement. Using the internal harness Toshi, he now breaks a feature plan into phases, spins up 10 parallel agents (mostly cheaper models) to execute, and gets a completion ping minutes later — the CEO is shipping features himself again.

By the numbers: 88% of Coinbase revenue already comes from non-Bitcoin trading; prediction markets hit a $100M revenue run rate within months of launching; tokenized stocks (real securities held 1:1 in custody, available outside the US first) target the roughly 4 billion people without brokerage access.
