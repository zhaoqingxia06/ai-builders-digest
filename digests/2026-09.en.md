**Boris Cherny**, Claude Code at Anthropic, showed **Sonnet 5.5** fixing a bug in **Claude Code**, noting it is 30% faster and uses 30% less usage.

**Thibault Sottiaux**, Codex and ChatGPT at OpenAI, laid out a major change: OpenAI is re-opening **Pro $200** subscriptions to new subscribers tomorrow, but recalculating usage so it nets out at half the dollar in API spend versus the old plan. He committed to not reintroducing the 5h limit, guaranteeing more work done over time as models get more efficient, and avoiding artificially inflated API list prices. He noted this week's **GPT-6 Sol** and **GPT-6 Luna** launched at 50% of their previous price, and teased more subscription additions tomorrow that won't draw on usage.

**Peter Yang**, who writes practical AI tutorials and interviews, pushed back on the fickle X narrative of "OpenAI getting mogged by Claude 5.5" or "Anthropic cooked by Codex," saying he is glad there are 2 (and soon more) competitors pushing the frontier. **Cat Wu**, Claude Code and Cowork at Anthropic, said **Sonnet 5.5** gets users roughly 30% more tasks done in **Claude Code** versus Sonnet 5, needing fewer tokens for the same work. She shared it raking a yard of leaves using tool calls, 24s faster than Sonnet 5 with 6K fewer tokens.

**Thariq**, Claude Code at Anthropic, argued it is basically impossible to just "show you their prompt" now because everything is about references, skills, and examples, often asking an agent to look at 3 other repos, search the web, and use other AI APIs. He said with **Sonnet** and **Opus 5.5** this intelligence should be very available, and to try **Sonnet 5.5** when making workflows.

**Guillermo Rauch**, CEO of Vercel, announced you can now search **Vercel** domains without auth, especially great for an agent. He also shared a migration that delivered ~70% faster builds and ~75% faster paints, deriving two AI skills from the work, with the net done in under a week.

**Alex Albert**, Research at Anthropic, said **Sonnet 5.5** has the same feel he liked about **Opus 5.5**: it writes clearly, is very fast, and is a major capabilities jump over Sonnet 5, a really great model to iterate with.

**Aaron Levie**, CEO of Box, shared early access testing of **Sonnet 5.5** on Box's complex work eval with the **Box Agent**. Across the board they saw a 4 pt overall improvement on their hardest tests, roughly 2.4× faster to a finished deliverable on 12% fewer tokens, with vertical wins of +18 percentage points in Financial Services, +7 pt in Legal, +8 pt in Life Sciences, and +7 pt in the Public Sector. Specific wins included a financial services due-diligence review (+48 points, 61% faster), a legal commercial lease review (+14 points), a life sciences field trial writeup (+22 points, 41% faster), and a public sector report (+11 points, 71% faster). Customers will be able to build AI agents with Sonnet 5.5 in Box AI Studio shortly.

**Dan Shipper**, CEO of Every, said **Sonnet 5.5** has dramatically improved writing even versus **Opus 5.5** in his testing, though Astra remains his favorite for writing, with Sonnet 5.5 beating it at revision tasks. It is faster and cheaper than Opus 5.5, making it the preferred model for quick iterative coding and design work, though some on his team feel there is no room for mid-tier models anymore.

**Sam Altman**, whose mission at OpenAI is to ensure AGI benefits all of humanity, said he is pretty excited for **DevDay** tomorrow and that "we have found a new thing."

**Nikunj Kothari**, partner at FPV Ventures, questioned the "distribution is a moat" investing thesis now that incumbents are flexing distribution muscle. He urged staying first principles, finding unfair advantages, building a great product with high retention and network effects, and using capital as a weapon to compound rather than as destiny.

**Zara Zhang**, a builder, is looking to chat with followers building web-based or frontend AI experiences that struggle to look beautiful, those seeing cool demos but unsure how to replicate them, and those creating demos willing to share their process.
